## Learning objective
- Learn how to buy accuracy at answer time, why picking the right try is the hard part, and how to spend tries by difficulty.

## Why test-time compute?
- Test-time compute: extra work spent when answering (more tries, revisions, search) instead of when training.
- No training needed. Same model, more tries.
- Two ways to spend it:
  - Parallel: many independent tries, then pick one
  - Sequential: one try, then revise it, then revise again
- The catch: something must tell the right try from the wrong ones.

## Repeated sampling and coverage
- Repeated sampling: ask the same question many times at temperature above 0, so the tries differ.
- Temperature 0: every try is the same. More tries add nothing.
- Coverage (pass@k): share of problems where at least one of k tries is right.
- Coverage is an upper bound. It assumes a perfect checker.

## Large Language Monkeys (Brown et al. 2024)
- Sampled up to 10,000 times per problem on math and coding sets.
- Coverage kept rising over four orders of magnitude.
- SWE-bench Lite, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct: 15.9% with 1 try → 56% with 250 tries.
  - Single-try state of the art at the time: 43%

## Cheap model + many tries vs expensive model
| 5 tries, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct | 1 try, GPT-4o |
|---|---|
| Solved 29.62% of SWE-bench Lite | Solved 24.00% |
| Cost $10.8 | Cost $39 |

- Many cheap tries can win, if a checker picks the right one.
- On SWE-bench, the checker is the test suite.

## Picking is the hard part
| Coverage (oracle picker) | Majority vote / reward model |
|---|---|
| MATH, Llama-3-8B-Instruct: 82.9% at 100 tries → 98.44% at 10,000 | Stalled around 100 tries: best gain 40.50% → 41.41% |

- Majority vote (self-consistency): keep the answer most tries agree on. Needs no checker, but stalls.
- Generation-verification gap: the model could produce the right answer, but the picker can't find it.
- Module a02 is about closing this gap.

## Why the curve flattens (Schaeffer et al. 2025)
- One problem: the chance all k tries fail drops fast (exponentially) as k grows.
- Average over many problems: drops slowly, like a power law.
- Heavy tail: a few very hard problems with near-zero success per try. They cause the slow average.
- Their exponent estimator: about 10x lower error than curve fitting.
- Lesson: sample a little, look at per-problem success rates, forecast before you spend.

## Archon: stack the tricks (Saad-Falcon et al. 2024)
- Generator: makes candidate answers.
- Ranker: orders candidates and keeps the best.
- Fuser: merges several candidates into one better answer.
- Verifier: keeps only answers whose reasoning checks out.
- Searched 9,576 stacks; the best beat o1, GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet by 15.1% on average.
  - Fuser helped on every benchmark (8.9% on average); verifiers helped most on reasoning.

## Spend by difficulty (Snell et al. 2024)
| Strategy | What it does | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential | One try, then revise it | Easy questions |
| Mix of both | Some tries, some revisions | Harder questions |
| Bigger model | Pay for size, not tries | Hardest questions, or heavy traffic |

- Difficulty: the model's own success rate over many tries, not a human label.
- Choosing per question matched or beat best-of-N with up to 4x less compute.

## Scoring majority vote in code
```python
def majority_acc(probs, k):
    return sum(majority([s["answer"] for s in SAMPLES[p["id"]][:k]]) == p["answer"] for p in probs) / len(probs)
```
- For each problem, take its first k sampled answers.
- `majority(...)`: keep the most common answer.
- Compare it to the true answer; count the hits.
- Divide by the number of problems: share solved by voting at k.

## How this applies to us
- A small local model is cheap to run many times on the firm's own machine.
- Contract checks often have a code checker: does the quote exist? Is the cap at most 12 months of fees?
- Where nothing can check the answer, more tries mostly add cost. Build the checker first.

## Common mistakes
- Reporting coverage as accuracy → report what your real picker gets.
- Adding samples without a checker → build verification first; voting stalls near 100 tries.
- Counting calls, not tokens → a fuser or revision call reads all candidates; count tokens.
- Tuning the strategy on the set you report on → hold out data for the final score.

## Check your understanding
**Q: Coverage at 100 tries is 90%, majority vote at 100 tries is 45%. What is missing?**
A: A good verifier. The model can produce the right answer, but the picker can't find it: the generation-verification gap.

**Q: Why does the average success curve flatten when each single problem is solved fast with more tries?**
A: A heavy tail of very hard problems with near-zero success per try pulls the average down, so it falls like a power law.

**Q: For an easy question, should you spend 4 calls on 4 parallel tries or 1 try plus 3 revisions?**
A: Snell et al. found revision best on easy questions. They used a fine-tuned revision model, so test it on your own task.
