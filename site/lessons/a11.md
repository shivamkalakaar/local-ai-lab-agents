## Direct answer vs chain of thought
| Direct | Chain of thought (CoT) |
|---|---|
| "Reply with only the final number, no words, no working." | "Think step by step, showing each calculation." |
| No room for the steps | Steps first, then `ANSWER: <number>` |
| 15 tokens max | 500 tokens max |

- CoT: the model writes its reasoning steps before the answer.
- Gain grows with model size; tiny models can write steps that look right but are wrong.

## Self-consistency
- Sample several reasoning paths (temperature above 0); take the majority answer.
- Many paths lead to the right answer; wrong answers tend to scatter.
- Agreement rate: share of samples matching the majority. A free confidence score.
- High agreement ≠ truth. A model can be wrong the same way every time.

## Voting in code
```python
samples = pmap(sample_answers, PROBS)
votes = [Counter(a for a in s if a is not None).most_common(1) for s in samples]
sc = [v[0][0] if v else None for v in votes]
agree = [v[0][1] / K if v else 0 for v in votes]
```
- Line 1: K = 5 CoT samples per problem at temperature 0.7.
- Line 2: count the final answers; find the most common.
- Line 3: the majority answer.
- Line 4: agreement = majority count / K.
- Review gate (Solution 3): agreement below 60% → send to a person.

## Least-to-most
- Stage 1: break the problem into simpler sub-questions. Don't solve yet.
- Stage 2: solve them in order; each answer feeds the next.
- For problems harder than the prompt examples.
- Our notebook: 2 calls (decompose, then solve all). The paper solves each sub-question in its own call.

## CoT-decoding: reasoning without a prompt
| Greedy decoding | CoT-decoding |
|---|---|
| Always pick the most likely next token | Branch on the top-k first tokens (k = 10 in the paper) |
| One path; often answers at once | Continue each branch greedily |
| Hides reasoning the model can do | Pick by answer confidence |

- Answer confidence (Δ): average gap between top-1 and top-2 token probabilities over the answer tokens.
- Needs raw completion and log-probs. Without them, our notebook uses branch agreement instead.

## Denny Zhou's four papers
| Paper | What it showed |
|---|---|
| Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al. 2022) | 540B model + 8 CoT examples: state of the art on GSM8K, beat fine-tuned GPT-3 with a verifier |
| Self-Consistency (Wang et al. 2022) | Over CoT: GSM8K +17.9%, SVAMP +11.0%, AQuA +12.2% |
| Least-to-Most (Zhou et al. 2022) | SCAN, 14 examples: at least 99% on every split vs 16% for CoT |
| CoT-decoding (Wang and Zhou 2024) | Pre-trained Mistral-7B on GSM8K: 9.9% greedy → 25.1% |

## Math olympiad: from formal to plain language
| System | Result |
|---|---|
| AlphaGeometry (2024) | 25 of 30 olympiad geometry problems; previous best 10; average gold medalist about 25.9 |
| AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry 2 (IMO 2024) | 28 of 42 points, silver; Lean proofs; some problems took up to three days |
| Gemini Deep Think (IMO 2025) | 35 of 42 points, gold; natural language, within 4.5 hours |

- Lean: a proof language a computer can check exactly.
- Parallel thinking: explore several candidate solutions at once.
- A checkable reward is what made the RL work.

- Agreement tells the firm which contract answers go straight through and which need a lawyer.
- The firm's labeled contracts are a checkable reward, at small scale.

## Common mistakes
- Forcing "only the number" on a multi-step problem → let it reason, grade the final answer with code.
- High agreement = correct → agreement measures consistency, not truth.
- CoT-decoding through the chat endpoint → needs raw completion and log-probs.
- Copying the IMO recipe without a checker → RL worked because answers could be verified exactly.

## Check your understanding
**Q: What is the difference between self-consistency and CoT-decoding?**
A: Self-consistency samples whole reasoning paths from a CoT prompt and votes. CoT-decoding uses no prompt, branches only on the first token, continues greedily and picks by answer confidence.

**Q: What made RL work in AlphaProof?**
A: Lean, a formal checker that verifies proofs exactly, gave a reliable reward.
